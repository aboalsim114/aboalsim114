# Regulation-driven software opportunities (EU and France, 2026-2030), status at 16 November 2026

Method note: 17 searches. Most results are secondary sources (law-firm notes, compliance-vendor blogs, comparison sites). Official hosts (economie.gouv.fr) could not be fetched from this session because the network blocks them, so official texts were checked only through what other sources quote. Figures marked "(secondary)" should be checked against EUR-Lex, Légifrance or impots.gouv.fr before they go into a final report.

## Q1. Which obligations hit many SMEs and micro-businesses soon, and which were delayed or watered down?

### Takeaway
French e-invoicing is by far the biggest and most certain obligation. It covers more than 7 million companies: all of them must be able to receive e-invoices since 1 September 2026, and SMEs, micro-businesses and auto-entrepreneurs must issue them from 1 September 2027. Most other candidates were postponed (AI Act high-risk rules, EUDR, NIS2 in France, French pay transparency), cut back (CSRD now covers only companies with more than 1,000 employees and €450M turnover, roughly 80% fewer companies) or are still years away (ViDA in 2030, Digital Product Passports for textiles around 2028-29). The obligations that are both firm and close are: e-invoicing issuance in Sept 2027, EUDR in Dec 2026 and June 2027, the battery passport in Feb 2027, AI Act transparency in Dec 2026 and high-risk rules in Dec 2027, and EAA enforcement, which is already under way.

### Cited Findings

**French e-invoicing and e-reporting (in force; the strongest market)**
- The reform took effect on 1 Sept 2026. Since then every company must be able to receive e-invoices, and large companies and ETIs must also issue them and send e-reporting data. Issuance for TPE, PME, micro-businesses and auto-entrepreneurs starts on 1 Sept 2027 — [economie.gouv.fr, as quoted in search results](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/actualites/facturation-electronique-les-entreprises-accompagnees-tout-au-long-du-deploiement); [libeo calendar](https://libeo.io/blog/calendrier-facture-electronique-2026)
- More than 7 million companies in France are covered — [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/tout-savoir-sur-la-facturation-electronique-pour-les-entreprises)
- About 500,000 companies had activated a reception address by 16 Jan 2026. Another figure says the central PPF directory listed more than 375,000 entities. The two figures date from different times — [libeo](https://libeo.io/blog/reforme-facture-electronique-2026); [abby](https://abby.fr/guide/facturation-electronique/pdp-facturation-electronique)
- Fines under the Loi de finances 2026 (loi n° 2026-103, art. 123): €50 per invoice not issued electronically (up from €15) and €500 per missing e-reporting transmission (up from €250). Each fine is capped at €15,000 per year, and the two caps are separate. Some sites (e.g. Shine) still show the old amounts — [comparatif-facture-electronique.fr](https://www.comparatif-facture-electronique.fr/calculer-amende-non-conformite-facturation-electronique-2026/); [CAPEB](https://www.capeb.fr/actualites/des-sanctions-plus-severes-en-cas-de-non-respect-de-la-facturation-electronique-vesr0i); [meilleurtaux, Oct 2026](https://banque.meilleurtaux.com/banque-en-ligne/actualites/2026-octobre/facture-electronique-amendes-delais-et-tolerance.html)

**EU AI Act after the Digital Omnibus on AI (adopted; high-risk rules postponed)**
- The Digital Omnibus on AI is Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. Political agreement came on 7 May 2026, Parliament voted on 16 June, the Council gave final approval on 29 June, it was published in the Official Journal on 24 July and it entered into force on 27 July 2026 — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/27/the-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force-today-102nedo)
- High-risk systems listed in Annex III (hiring, credit scoring, education) now apply from 2 Dec 2027 instead of 2 Aug 2026. AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I) applies from 2 Aug 2028 instead of 2 Aug 2027 — [European Post](https://theeuropeanpost.eu/european-affairs/digital-omnibus-on-ai-delays-high-risk-rules-to-december-2027/); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus)
- The Article 50 transparency duties were not postponed, including the 2 Dec 2026 deadline for watermarking and labelling AI-generated content — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)
- The AI literacy duty in Article 4 was softened. Providers and deployers no longer have to "ensure" literacy; they must "take measures to support" it, which is an obligation of effort rather than of result — [KI-Campus](https://ki-campus.org/en/blog/eu-ai-act-ai-omnibus-recalibrates-article-4-ai-literacy-remains-mandatory)

**CSRD and VSME after Omnibus I (heavily cut back)**
- Omnibus I is Directive (EU) 2026/470, in force from 18 March 2026 according to secondary sources. The duty to report now applies only to companies with more than 1,000 employees on average AND net turnover of at least €450M. The Commission estimates this removes about 80% of companies from direct scope — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1f627ab7-e226-4ad6-81fd-d934e700b34a); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251222-eu-sustainability-omnibus-i-detailed-omnibus); [Projet Celsius](https://projetcelsius.com/en/blog/csrd-2026-who-is-in-scope-after-omnibus/)
- VSME has been renamed "VS" (Voluntary Standard) and was adopted by the Commission as a delegated act on 3 July 2026 (C(2026) 5011). It is voluntary for companies with up to 1,000 employees. From 2027 it also works as a "value-chain cap": large companies cannot ask suppliers for more ESG information than the VS covers. Sources disagree on whether it entered into force on 24 Sept 2026 or was still in the scrutiny period — [Linklaters](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102n88h/eu-csrd-commission-adopts-revised-esrs-and-voluntary-reporting-standard); [Rödl](https://www.roedl.com/en/insights/the-voluntary-standard-vs-for-esg-requirements-benefits-and-implementation/); [Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/at/en/services/sustainability/sustainability-blog-beitraege/vs-instead-of-vsme-the-new-standard)

**EUDR (postponed twice; dates now firm)**
- Regulation (EU) 2025/2650 of 19 Dec 2025 sets the application date at 30 Dec 2026 for large and medium operators and for all traders. Micro and small operators that were established as such by 31 Dec 2024 have until 30 June 2027. Micro and small operators handling timber products already covered by the old Timber Regulation stay on the Dec 2026 date — [Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/the-eu-deforestation-regulation-eudr-what-businesses-need-to-know-and-do); [MAAK law](https://www.maak-law.com/product-compliance-netherlands/eudr-agreement-postponement-application-date/)
- The Commission's review of 4 May 2026 kept these dates. A delegated act will adjust which commodities and products are in scope; removing leather has been proposed — [Global ELR](https://www.globalelr.com/2026/05/european-commission-releases-new-eu-deforestation-regulation-measures/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Regulation_on_Deforestation-free_products)

**NIS2 in France ("loi Résilience"; not yet adopted)**
- The bill passed the Senate on 12 March 2025 and the special committee of the Assemblée nationale in Sept 2025. Its debate in the chamber, set to start on 7 Oct 2026, was postponed again the day before. There is no promulgated law and no implementing decree — [Myra Security, Oct 2026](https://www.myrasecurity.com/fr/savoir/nis2-loi-resilience/); [nis-solutions.eu](https://nis-solutions.eu/countries/france/)
- On 8 July 2026 the European Commission referred France, Ireland, Spain and the Netherlands to the Court of Justice for missing the transposition deadline — [cyber-diagnostic.fr](https://cyber-diagnostic.fr/nis2-france/)
- ANSSI published a working version of its Référentiel Cyber France (ReCyF) on 17 March 2026 and opened voluntary pre-registration on MonEspaceNIS2 on 24 Nov 2025. According to an Oct 2026 hearing quoted second-hand, ANSSI aims for full compliance by the end of 2028 — [Myra Security](https://www.myrasecurity.com/fr/savoir/nis2-loi-resilience/)
- About 15,000 entities in 18 sectors would be covered in France, up from about 500 under NIS1; this comes from the bill's explanatory note as quoted by others. Other estimates range from 10,000-15,000 to 15,000-20,000 — [Kiteworks](https://www.kiteworks.com/fr/?p=258118); [Legiscope](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/transposition-nis2-france.html); [FrenchWeb](https://www.frenchweb.fr/les-15-000-entreprises-qui-decouvriront-nis-2-apres-le-vote/462987)
- A Senate document mentions about 1,500 local authorities in the wider scope. This was not confirmed elsewhere — [Sénat](https://conferenceconsensuslogement.senat.fr/rap/l24-393/l24-393-syn.pdf)

**Pay transparency directive 2023/970 (France has missed the deadline)**
- The EU transposition deadline was 7 June 2026. France sent a draft to the social partners on 6 March 2026, then to the Conseil d'État. The bill was tabled in the Senate on 10 Sept 2026 (n° 944) under the fast-track procedure — [GHR](https://www.ghr.fr/social/actualites/transparence-salariale-la-transposition-se-precise-faisons-le-point); [Deloitte Avocats](https://blog.avocats.deloitte.fr/directive-pay-transparency-la-france-franchit-une-nouvelle-etape/); [info.gouv.fr](https://www.info.gouv.fr/actualite/transparence-des-salaires-regles-et-evolutions-prevues)
- Planned measures include a salary or salary range before hiring (in the job ad), a ban on asking candidates about past pay, and an end to pay-confidentiality clauses. Reports conflict on whether the French bill applies the new pay index from 50 employees (the directive's mandatory reporting starts at 100). Adoption may slip to 2027 and application to early 2028; one commentator thinks adoption before the presidential election is "compromise" — [GHR](https://www.ghr.fr/social/actualites/transparence-salariale-la-transposition-se-precise-faisons-le-point); [Mozaik](https://www.mozaik.co/transparence-salariale-dates-application-france/)
- Directive text: pay-gap reporting starts in 2027 for employers with 250+ employees (yearly) and 150-249 (every 3 years), and in 2031 for 100-149. Pay information for job candidates applies to all employers — [EUR-Lex 2023/970](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/970/oj) (from the directive's text; dates as recalled, verify)

**European Accessibility Act (in force since 28 June 2025; enforcement starting)**
- France transposed it through Law 2023-171 and Decree 2023-931. The DGCCRF enforces it in the private sector and Arcom handles public-sector RGAA. Sanctions are 5th-class contraventions (art. R.451-4 C. conso). Sources disagree sharply on maximum amounts: €7,500 per company plus €3,000 per day, €50,000 per product, or €250,000 — [vread.fr sources](https://outils.vread.fr/declaration-accessibilite/en/sources/); [RGAA Test](https://rgaa-test.fr/en/guide/sanctions-accessibilite-2025-2026); [libration.io](https://libration.io/en/resources/eaa-fines-enforcement/)
- Enforcement so far: in Nov 2025 the DGCCRF sent formal notices to Auchan, Carrefour, E.Leclerc and Picard. About 50 e-commerce businesses have been examined since Jan 2026; by Sept 2026 more than 20 sites had been checked, half of them with follow-up planned. A Lille court dismissed a claim on 5 May 2026 and an appeal is pending. As of 19 Oct 2026 no EAA fine had been published in 14 tracked countries. Final DGCCRF results on e-commerce are expected early 2027 — [ComplyLoft tracker](https://www.complyloft.com/accessibility/eaa/enforcement); [libration.io](https://libration.io/en/resources/eaa-fines-enforcement/)
- Micro-enterprises that provide services (fewer than 10 staff and turnover or balance sheet of €2M or less) are exempt from the EAA's service requirements — [Directive (EU) 2019/882, art. 4(5)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj)

**Digital Product Passport (ESPR and batteries)**
- The battery passport applies from 18 Feb 2027 (Battery Regulation 2023/1542, art. 77). It covers EV batteries, batteries for light means of transport, and industrial batteries above 2 kWh — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_passport); [PassportCraft](https://passportcraft.com/insights/eu-battery-passport-guide)
- No ESPR product-group delegated act had been adopted as of Oct-Nov 2026. The textiles act is expected around late 2027, with obligations about 18 months later, so around 2028-29. The first acts have already slipped — [dpp-tool.com, Nov 2026](https://dpp-tool.com/en/blog/espr-delegated-acts/); [PassportCraft timeline](https://passportcraft.com/insights/dpp-timeline-2026-2030-every-deadline)

**ViDA (VAT in the Digital Age; adopted, applies from 2030)**
- Adopted by the Council on 11 March 2025 and published in the Official Journal on 25 March 2025. E-invoicing and digital reporting become mandatory for intra-EU B2B transactions from 1 July 2030, and existing national systems must be aligned by 2035. The Commission published an implementation work programme on 20 May 2026. Sources disagree on whether the reporting window is 5 or 10 days — [Revenue.ie timeline](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vat/vida-vat-modernisation/vida-implementation-timeline.aspx); [Edicom](https://edicomgroup.com/blog/vida-the-european-union-promotes-b2b-electronic-invoicing); [VATCalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/vat/eu-transaction-based-reporting-tbr-vat-options/)

**French DPE rental bans for energy-inefficient housing**
- Homes rated G cannot be newly let since 1 Jan 2025. The ban extends to F on 1 Jan 2028 and to E in 2034 in mainland France. Overseas territories follow later dates (G in 2028, F in 2031) — [Selectra](https://selectra.info/energie/actualites/marche/passoires-thermiques-calendrier-interdiction-location); [CPIM](https://www.cpim.fr/dpe-2028-derniere-ligne-droite/)
- About 600,000 G homes and 1.2 to 1.5 million F homes are affected, depending on the source. A new electricity conversion coefficient (1.9 from 1 Jan 2026) moved about 750,000 homes up one class. A housing bill passed by the Senate would allow F and G homes to be let again if the owner signs up to carry out works; it was not yet promulgated — [Selectra 2026](https://selectra.info/energie/actualites/renovation-energetique/2026-dpe-maprimerenov-interdiction-location); [Selectra Sénat](https://selectra.info/energie/actualites/renovation-energetique/loi-relance-logement-senat-vote-passoires-relocation-2026)

**Whistleblowing channels**
- Private employers with 50 or more workers must set up internal reporting channels — [Directive (EU) 2019/1937, art. 8](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/1937/oj). In force since 2021-2023, so this is a mature market rather than an upcoming deadline.

### Inferences
- Ranking of opportunities, judged on companies obligated × certainty × deadline × penalties × room for a newcomer:
  1. **E-invoicing for micro-businesses, TPE and verticals (France, issuance from Sept 2027).** Millions of companies, a firm date and fines per invoice. But supply is crowded (about 165 approved platforms) and free plans exist, so the realistic angle is vertical tools (trades and construction, health professions, farming, associations, cross-border sellers) or add-ons (PA connectors, e-reporting for B2C sellers and e-commerce, invoice-data cleaning) rather than a generic PA.
  2. **AI Act compliance for deployers and SME providers (Art. 50 in Dec 2026, Annex III in Dec 2027).** HR tech, credit and education firms need inventories, risk management, documentation and transparency labelling. The deadline is firm now, which helps, but the softer literacy duty weakens "AI training" as an offer.
  3. **EUDR due diligence for SMEs and traders (Dec 2026 / June 2027).** Many importers and traders of wood, cocoa, coffee, soy, rubber, cattle and palm oil. Vendor prices are mostly not published (one listing starts at about $675 per year), and supply for micro-operators in French and other non-English languages looks thin.
  4. **NIS2 compliance for mid-sized entities and local authorities in France (about 15,000 entities, law pending, compliance aimed at end-2028).** Large volume but uncertain timing. Products tied to ReCyF (gap analysis, incident reporting, supplier management) in French.
  5. **Accessibility (EAA) audits and monitoring for e-commerce and service SMEs.** Enforcement is real but there are no fines yet, and service micro-businesses are exempt. Overlay tools are controversial, so audit-plus-fix workflows in French are the more credible route.
  6. **Pay transparency (applies around 2027-28 in France).** Pay ranges in job ads affect every employer that hires; pay-gap reports affect companies with 100+ employees. Payroll and HRIS incumbents (PayFit and others) will likely bundle it, so a standalone tool is risky.
  7. **Battery passport (Feb 2027), and ESPR passports later (2028+).** A small number of obligated firms in batteries, with textiles a larger wave around 2028-29. Fits a B2B company targeting mid-sized manufacturers.
  8. **VS/VSME reports for SMEs answering requests from large clients.** The obligation is gone; demand now comes only from the value-chain cap and from banks. Smaller and more price-sensitive than expected before Omnibus.
  9. **DPE tools for landlords and property managers (F ban in 2028).** 1.2-1.5M homes affected, but the rule may be loosened by the housing bill.
  10. **ViDA intra-EU e-reporting (2030).** Too far away for a 2026 start-up's first market, but a natural extension for an e-invoicing product.

### Gaps
- No official count of companies by size in France (INSEE) was retrieved to size the micro-business share of the 7M+.
- The NIS2 count of about 15,000 comes from the bill's explanatory note as quoted by others; the primary document was not seen. No EU-wide number was found.
- No count of EU or French operators covered by EUDR was found; the Commission's impact assessment is needed.
- Maximum EAA sanctions in France are contradictory across sources (€7.5k, €50k, €250k).
- The 50 vs 100 employee threshold in the French pay-transparency bill was not confirmed.
- The exact in-force date of VS/VSME (24 Sept 2026 or later) was not confirmed.
- The Data Act (applies from 12 Sept 2025), DAC7/DAC8 (DAC8 crypto reporting from 1 Jan 2026) and Loi AGEC/REP were not researched in this pass, so no cited facts are available for them.

## Q2. How many companies are obligated, what are the fines, which tools exist at what price, and where is supply thin?

### Takeaway
E-invoicing has the volume (more than 7M companies) and the clearest fines (€50 per invoice and €500 per e-reporting transmission, each capped at €15k per year). It is also the most saturated market: about 165 approved platforms, a median price around €24 per month, and 26% of platforms offering a permanent free plan. Supply is thinner, and prices higher or unpublished, in EUDR, AI Act and NIS2 compliance for SMEs, especially in French.

### Cited Findings
- Approved e-invoicing platforms (PA, formerly PDP) went from 61 at the first publication in Sept 2024 to 101 in Jan 2026, more than 120 in April 2026 and 165 out of 178 applicants by 13 Aug 2026 (third-party count) — [comparatif-facture-electronique.fr](https://www.comparatif-facture-electronique.fr/plateformes-agreees/); [infos-entreprises.fr](https://infos-entreprises.fr/2026/05/05/plateformes-agreees-pdp-reforme-2026/)
- In May 2026, across 68 audited approved platforms, the median price was €24 per month and 26% offered a permanent free plan. For a micro-business, reception only costs €0-10 per month and regular sending plus receiving costs €10-30 per month — [ma-facture-electronique.org prices](https://ma-facture-electronique.org/plateforme-agreee/prix-tarifs/); [tool-advisor.fr](https://tool-advisor.fr/logiciel-facturation/prix-plateforme-agreee/)
- Free approved-platform plans: Indy (approved by the DGFiP on 9 Jan 2026), Abby "Essentiel" at €0 (paid plans from €7.20 per month) and Tiime "Free" — [tool-advisor.fr](https://tool-advisor.fr/logiciel-facturation/comparatif/plateforme-agreee-gratuite/); [Indy](https://www.indy.fr/guide/facturation/electronique/cout/?swcfpc=1); [Tiime](https://blog.tiime.fr/cout-facturation-electronique-2026)
- EUDR tools: most vendors do not publish prices. One listing shows about $675 per year as a starting point. Vendors include osapiens, Coolset and others — [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/product/526665-EUDR-Compliance-System); [Coolset tools list](https://www.coolset.com/nl/academy/best-6-eudr-compliance-tools-for-2026-supply-chain-due-diligence)
- Fines: e-invoicing as above. NIS2 under the directive: up to €10M or 2% of worldwide turnover for essential entities and €7M or 1.4% for important entities — [Copla](https://copla.com/blog/compliance-regulations/nis2-directive-regulations-and-implementation-in-france/) (directive figures; the French law is not adopted). EAA in France: amounts are contradictory (see Q1).

### Inferences
- In e-invoicing the base product is commoditised and often free, sold by accounting software, neobanks and banks. A newcomer would need a wedge: a vertical, a hard document type (construction progress invoices, subcontracting, self-billing), B2C e-reporting for e-commerce and marketplaces, reconciliation or collections built on structured invoice data, or tools for accounting firms.
- Markets with unpublished, enterprise-style prices (EUDR, AI Act, NIS2) leave room for transparent self-serve SME pricing at €50-300 per month. That is an inference, not checked against vendor price lists.

### Gaps
- No public price lists were collected for AI Act governance tools, NIS2/GRC tools for French SMEs, accessibility audit tools or pay-transparency modules.
- No count of how many approved platforms target micro-businesses specifically.

## Q3. What evidence exists of startups already making money from these obligations?

### Takeaway
The clearest evidence is in French e-invoicing and accounting. Pennylane raised €75M in April 2025, explicitly framed around the reform, and a later €175M round is reported. No 2026 funding rounds tied to NIS2, CSRD or accessibility were found in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Pennylane raised €75M in April 2025 to scale up ahead of the e-invoicing reform — [Maddyness](https://www.maddyness.com/2025/04/07/pennylane-leve-75-millions-deuros-a-lapproche-de-la-reforme-de-la-facturation-electronique/)
- A later Pennylane round of €175M is listed in Maddyness coverage. The date and details were not verified — [Maddyness top 2025](https://www.maddyness.com/2026/01/06/french-tech-voici-les-10-levees-de-fonds-les-plus-importantes-de-2025/)
- LegalPlace raised €70M in 2026 and acquired Legalstart (legal admin for SMEs, linked to compliance but not to a single regulation) — [Maddyness, June 2026](https://www.maddyness.com/2026/06/02/french-tech-en-mai-les-startups-ont-leve-deux-fois-plus-que-lannee-derniere/)
- Pennylane is running referral promotions on comparison sites ("3 mois offerts", code PENNY3), a sign of aggressive customer acquisition around the deadline — [ma-facture-electronique.org](https://ma-facture-electronique.org/reforme-2026/sanctions-amendes/)

### Inferences
- The e-invoicing deadline has already pulled large venture rounds into general accounting platforms, which makes a head-on generic attack unlikely to work for a small new company. The remaining room is in niches the incumbents serve poorly.
- Funding signals for EUDR (osapiens, Coolset), ESG (Greenly, Sweep) and AI governance exist from memory but were not confirmed with sources in this pass. The report writer should not state amounts.

### Gaps
- Revenues of French compliance start-ups (Pennylane ARR, Indy, Tiime, Dastra and others) were not found.
- No Sifted or Les Echos funding data was retrieved for EUDR, NIS2, AI Act or accessibility start-ups.
